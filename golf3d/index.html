How to play

Mini golf, three dimensions, four clubs. Ninety seconds of reading.

  1. Pull, and let go. Press anywhere on the course and pull away from where you want the ball to go, like a catapult. How far you pull is how hard you hit — the ring round the ball fills up as it winds on, and turns red when there is nothing left to give. The direction is simply the reverse of your pull, so one drag aims anywhere, all the way round, and the camera holds still while you do it. Let go to play the shot.
  2. Pick a club. The bag is under the course, or keys 1–4. The putter rolls flat and stops where you tell it, the driver is the reach club, the chipper hops a rail and keeps running, and the wedge goes up and over water and sand. Each one has its own ceiling on power, so a full swing means something different in every hand.
  3. Read the hole first. V lifts the camera above the whole hole; scroll or pinch to zoom. The dotted line in front of the ball is where the shot starts out — it is real physics, so it tells you the truth about rails and ramps.
  4. Mind the water. A splash or a fall off the edge costs one stroke and you play again from where you hit. Sand is heavy going, boards are quick, and a tilted green breaks the ball downhill the whole way.
