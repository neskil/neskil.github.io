Paper Plane Glider

Grab a corner and drag it where it should go: the flap swings over and creases, mirrored on the other side. Your folds become a list you can reorder, tune and delete, and the sheet re-folds itself every time, with a live 3D model and a test throw beside it. Start from the Classic Dart, Wide Glider or Nakamura Lock, or a blank sheet. The flight is worked out from the paper you folded, in 3D: a smaller wing makes it veer, a tilted throw curves it, and the walls, desks and pillars are solid. Then shape the wings and throw: ten missions, the longest throw or today's bin.

📄 3 designs or your own • 10 missions • daily bin

Pocket Orbit

Thrust the way you are going and the far side of your orbit rises; to catch something ahead of you, slow down. Seven missions teach it by doing it, from lifting periapsis out of the air through Hohmann transfers to moving stations and a delivery round, to a capture around the Moon. The dotted path is the one you will fly, ghosts show where you and your target will meet, and "Show me" hands the controls to an autopilot that narrates as it flies. Earth is NASA's Blue Marble, seen from above the pole.

Sound of Your Name

Every letter is always the same note, so names that share letters share phrases. Vowels are held, consonants move on, and an å gets a grace note. The name as a whole picks the key, mode, tempo, chords and instrument. Played live with Web Audio and drawn as a rosette that follows the music.

Pocket Black Hole

Turn it, zoom in, and switch gravity's bending off to see the same scene in flat space. The disc's near side outshines the far one because the gas is doing half the speed of light. Slide the mass from one Sun to tens of billions and the picture turns into kilometres and lap times, and an inset lets you fire light past the hole to find the edge of the shadow by hand.

Gait Lab

Every creature in a race has the same body and a different set of reflexes: when each muscle pulls, how hard, how fast the beat, which feet grip. Sixty race for ten seconds, the best go through, and the rest are children of the better half with a few mistakes copied in. Watch every race or skip 25 generations at a time, then draw a body of your own and share it by link.

Rainmaker

Each raindrop is followed downhill on its own. Pour a cloudburst under your finger, push up a hill or dig a channel, and flip back to see the island as it was before the weather.
